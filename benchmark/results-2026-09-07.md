# Baseline results — 2026-09-07

Harness: `bench_mux.py` in this folder (see `README.md` for method).
Raw data: `results-2026-09-07.json` (per-op min/p50/p90/max/mean/stdev,
plus `payload_bytes_mean` for attach/capture ops and `heavy/retained-bytes`).
Host: Linux 6.8.0-138-generic x86_64, tmux 3.4, aplexer 0.1.4,
Python 3.14.3. Defaults: 25 iterations, 25 sessions present for
`list`, 6 roundtrips, 5 attach cycles, 4 MiB heavy flood.

## Takeaways

1. **Hot path wins.** `send`, `status`, `capture`, roundtrip, and
   `attach` are at parity or faster than raw tmux — attach holds at
   ~4–7 ms whether the session is idle or holding megabytes, so the
   20 ms chord-to-first-byte budget in
   `docs/fast-session-switching-design.md` has ample headroom, and bulk
   capture is ~10x faster than `capture-pane` (ring dump vs grid
   formatting).
2. **Lifecycle ops are the gap.** `a start` (~150 ms) and `a kill`
   (~82 ms at grace-0) trail `new-session`/`kill-session` by ~6x.
   The 2097 ms default-grace row is intentional policy (TERM, wait
   2000 ms, escalate), not mechanism cost — but note `bash -l` ate
   the full grace here, i.e. TERM did not end the workload and the
   path escalated. See `PLAN.md`.
3. **tmuxctl is 16–110x slower than raw tmux on every op**
   (Python startup + per-session server fan-out; `list` scans 25
   sockets). aplexer beats it 10–50x on the same ops — the
   isolate-everything architecture does not have to cost that much.
4. **`a list` table rendering costs ~7 ms over `--json`**
   (20.3 vs 13.4). Both trail `list-sessions` (6.2); the scan itself
   is the next place to look after create/kill.
5. **Attach p90 tail.** `attach/a-attach-detach` p90/max is 36.6 ms
   against a 7.4 ms p50 (one slow sample of five). Worth a re-run
   with more attach iterations before concluding anything.

## Caveats

- One host, one run, loaded box: absolute numbers move ±2x between
  runs (e.g. `tmux new-session` was 9 ms p50 in an earlier quiet run
  vs 26 ms here; `tmuxctl create` 491 → 867 ms). Ratios are the
  stable signal, not absolutes.
- `switch` is not directly benched (no in-process switch to drive
  yet); per the design doc it is resolve + handshake, bounded above
  by the ~7 ms attach number.
- Rerun with `python3 benchmark/bench_mux.py --json-out results-<date>.json`.
